Flavours you grew up with

India has had functional drinks for centuries. We put two of them in a can.

Kokum after lunch

On the Konkan coast a glass of kokum comes after every heavy meal: tart, cooling and deep pink. We steep the sun-dried rind cold with kola nut, a little jaggery and black salt.

Guava, chilli and salt

The cart outside every school cut guava into four and rubbed it with red chilli and salt. We press pink guava with fire-roasted Guntur chilli, lime and rock salt, then add the fizz.

What goes in the can

  1. Kokum rind, dried for a week

    Picked by hand in April and May by 140 farming families around Sindhudurg, then dried on woven mats in the sun.

  2. Pink guava, pressed in a day

    Winter fruit from orchards around Prayagraj, picked November to February and cold-pressed within 24 hours.

From the farm to your fridge

Three states, one brewery, and the road to wherever you are.

  • Sindhudurg, Maharashtra

    Kokum trees grow along the red laterite coast. The fruit is ready when it turns deep purple.

  • Seven days in the sun

    Only the rind goes into the can. The seeds go back to the farmers, who press them for kokum butter.

  • Guntur, Andhra Pradesh

    Chilli is graded by hand before it goes anywhere near a fire.

  • Two weeks on tarps

    Dried in the open until it snaps, then roasted over wood in small lots.

  • Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh

    The winter crop has the pinkest flesh. We buy it straight from the orchards.

  • Pune, Maharashtra

    Brewed, carbonated and canned in small batches, about 4,000 cans a day.

  • Your city

    One to five days anywhere in India. Next day in Mumbai, Pune, Bengaluru and Delhi NCR.
